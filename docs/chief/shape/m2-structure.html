How the archive organizes itself

You asked how to fit the big leagues, small cups, WCG, showmatches and non-match material into one site without it collapsing. Below is what four kinds of real archives do, the data shape that follows from it, and three ways the navigation could look. Pick one at the bottom.

1What other archives teach

Folders for "where it lives", filters for "finding it"

Libraries and museums file things in one shallow tree (collection → series → item), because a thing can only sit in one place. Then they add facets: independent labels like year, kind or subject you can mix freely. One tree can't also answer "everything with BoxeR in it", so that comes from labels and links, never folders.

SAA glossary · faceted classification

Only recurring things get a series

Liquipedia gives OSL one series page with every season under it, and keeps separate Major and Minor tournament lists plus a showmatch list. A one-off cup isn't forced into a family: it just carries a tier and a year. That's your "quick menus for the big leagues, listing for the rest".

Major · Minor

A series has many names

MusicBrainz treats a festival or award as a Series with aliases and ordered members. OSL is that: "2001 SKY", "Coca-Cola", "Olympus" are season names, the series identity never changes. Sponsor names live on the season, not the series.

MusicBrainz Series

One flexible item beats many special types

Discogs and MusicBrainz don't invent a new structure per thing; they use a few entity types joined by typed links ("interview about BoxeR", "photo from 2001 SKY OSL final"). New kinds of material = a new word in a list, not a redesign. That covers "whatever I later deem worth archiving".

MusicBrainz schema · Discogs masters

Search can stay serverless for a long time

Up to roughly 5–8 thousand games, one prebuilt list filtered in the browser gives exact filter counts instantly. Past that, Pagefind (free, static) loads only the slice it needs and has live filter counts built in. Build one search shape now so the swap later is invisible.

Pagefind filters · size threshold is our estimate, to spike-test

Not being the bottleneck

Big wikis let anyone propose, but approving is a granted right, and machines check the boring parts. For us: automatic checks on every submission now; trusted reviewers per era/league via GitHub's CODEOWNERS once people have a track record; a no-git submission form only when non-coders show up. Voting systems need hundreds of editors, so skip them.

MusicBrainz editing · CODEOWNERS

2The shape that follows

Five kinds of record. Arrows are links, not folders: one interview can point at a player, a match and an event at once, and shows up on all three pages.

Tier is a hand-set label (big / mid / small) and needs one written rule so it stays consistent. Proposed rule: big = televised individual or team league from OGN/MBC Game, or WCG; mid = other broadcast cups and invitationals; small = online and community cups. It decides what's prominent, never what's allowed in.

A · Big leagues as a menu bar

The league row under the header is always there; tap OSL and its seasons drop down. "All events" is the listing for everything else. Find is an icon that opens the full filter page.

  • Good: fastest route for the 80% case (someone hunting an OSL final).
  • Good: closest to your own instinct and to Liquipedia.
  • Cost: small cups and items sit one tap deeper; the menu bar grows if many series earn a slot.

B · Everything on one timeline

Home is the history itself: each year lists its big events as full-width bands, and small cups, showmatches and items as chips under them. League shortcuts are filters on the same timeline, not separate pages.

  • Good: a WCG run or a tiny cup sits right next to the OSL of the same year; nothing is second-class.
  • Good: reads as an archive of an era, good for newcomers.
  • Cost: long scroll once there are 15 years of content; returning fans need the jump bar to go straight to a league.

C · Search is the front door

Like a library catalog: a big search box with filter chips on top, then tiles for the big leagues and two catch-all collections. Every page you reach is really a saved search.

  • Good: scales best to tens of thousands of games; one system for everything.
  • Good: "Cups & invitationals" and "Stories & media" get equal billing with OSL.
  • Cost: less sense of place and history; a newcomer who doesn't know names has to start from tiles.

All three keep your Broadcast look and the race-matrix Find page; they differ in what greets you and how leagues are reached.

4Your answers

Q11 · Which front door?

My pick: A, with B's timeline as the "Browse by year" section on home. It matches your instinct (quick league menus + a listing for the rest), and the timeline keeps small events and WCG visible instead of buried. C's search lives behind the ⌕ either way.

Q12 · The data shape in section 2

Yes: series only for recurring leagues, every event tagged with tier/kind/year, one flexible "item" for everything that isn't a match.

Q13 · The tier rule in the yellow box

Yes as written; you can move any event between tiers by hand later.
